Media opinion manipulation:
The use of targeted propaganda is, of course, ancient. The development of social sciences and psychological research raised these possibilities to a new level of systematic application about 100 years ago. One of the first significant historical examples of the effectiveness of the new scientific propaganda was the entry of the USA into the First World War. When re-elected in the fall of 1916, President Woodrow Wilson had promised voters to keep the USA out of the First World War. However, by then, Britain and France had become heavily indebted to American banks for their war efforts. Since the loss of the war subsequently seemed possible for the two European powers, the lending US capitalist factions knew how to make provisions. They organized a huge public campaign to persuade Wilson to enter the war against the Central Powers. This happened in April 1917. This ensured the repayment of the interest-bearing loans after the end of the war. A good read on war propaganda from the First World War to the Iraq War in 2003 is the book by Anne Morelli, The Principles of War Propaganda.

Freedom of speech is highly selective. How Austria restricts solidarity with Palestinians
Under this title, Amnesty International published a detailed report in March 2026, analyzing the undermining of the fundamental rights to assembly and freedom of expression by official Austria between October 2023 and October 2025 in the context of solidarity with Palestine. It documents the increase in racism, particularly anti-Muslim and anti-Palestinian racism, gives activists and NGOs a voice, and above all accuses the Austrian rulers of acting in a manner hostile to human rights!

This well-documented accusation concerns the demand "From the river to the sea, Palestine will be free", used by decree as initial suspicion of "approval of terrorist acts", but not only. It also specifically targets the political instrumentalization of the IHRA definition of anti-Semitism to repress pro-Palestinian people, as well as the political criminalization of BDS and its legitimate, nonviolent forms of expression.

At the end of the report, concrete demands are made to the federal government:

• To revoke the IHRA definition and all associated guidelines and decisions, to revise the National Strategy against Anti-Semitism, and to implement a National Action Plan against Racism including "anti-Muslim and anti-Palestinian racisms and anti-Semitism" (based on the Jerusalem Declaration against Anti-Semitism)

• "To refrain from restricting freedom of expression, including slogans, use of symbols or other forms of expression in support of Palestinians that contradict Austria's international human rights obligations"

• "To withdraw any form of undue restrictions on the work of human rights defenders and associations so that they can operate in a safe and conducive environment. Protection from all forms of stigmatization and ensuring they have non-discriminatory access to public funds"

Particularly important are the demands to the Federal Ministry of Justice:

• "The repeal of the decree concerning the slogan 'From the River to the Sea, Palestine will be free' and the official declaration that it is null and void."

• "The examination of any restriction on the right to freedom of expression on a case-by-case basis – while maintaining a fair procedure and in accordance with international human rights norms and standards."

Noteworthy are the demands to the federal government, the city of Vienna, and the city of Graz, which have adopted corresponding resolutions against BDS:

• "Respecting and protecting the right to advocate for boycotts, divestment, and sanctions as a legitimate form of political expression that should not be prohibited or criminalized under human rights law, including by

• Refraining from non-binding resolutions that consider the boycott, divestment, and sanctions movement as categorically anti-Semitic.

• Ensuring that supporters of BDS can express their views and advance their campaigns without harassment, risk of prosecution or criminalization, or other measures that violate the right to freedom of expression.
