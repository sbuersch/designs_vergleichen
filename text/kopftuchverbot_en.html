Instead of the much-expected solutions to Austria's economic decline, our government has felt "compelled" to solve a problem that de facto does not exist. They want to free the poor girls from the burden of a supposedly forced headscarf. And apparently, according to the government, liberation works best by adding a new compulsion to the statute book: namely the compulsion to take off the headscarf.


Statistics on how many girls actually wear a headscarf involuntarily? They don't exist. Ask the girls themselves for their opinion? Not necessary. This is a law that is so far removed from any lived reality – as is usually the case when the ÖVP begins to intervene in social life with its "cunning peasant logic".


Why does the ÖVP even want such a law? Of course, one can only speculate about this: It cannot be ruled out that a woman like Claudia Bauer curiously actually believes that this would contribute to social progress. Another (and more likely) possibility is that this is once again about symbolic politics to appease incited right-wing voters. Firstly, for their own voters, who also notice that Austria is on a downward slide – at least this means "something" is being done, completely regardless of whether it changes their own situation or not. And secondly, for voters of potential other parties along the lines of: "Look at us, we are just as racist." After all, all parties are currently trying to claim this for themselves in one form or another.


Behind this lies the attempt to distract from actual problems such as social cuts and wage theft through racist division. It is about stirring up enemy images. In Austrian history, it was Jewish people who were stigmatized, marginalized and ultimately persecuted; today it is Muslim fellow citizens. Anti-Muslim racism is being stirred up on various levels: through headscarf bans, an Islam map on which all Muslim institutions in the country are marked, through media equating Islam with terror, through reports from the Austrian Integration Fund about the lack of willingness to integrate among certain groups, through mandated values courses and much more. However, the promotion of anti-Muslim racism also serves to secure the complicity of our government with the genocidal Israel. Israel tries to portray itself as a progressive Jewish state that fights for "us" against the bastion of hostile Islam. Or as the German Chancellor Merz put it: "Does the dirty work for us" when he tried to justify Israel's genocide and wars of aggression.


But what can be done about it now?


In cities like Vienna, the first informal action committees of teachers, pupils and parents have already formed, calling for a boycott of the law. In other parts of Austria, too, teachers have spoken out who are not willing to report female students because of their clothing. It will depend on the number of teachers who get involved whether they themselves have to fear civil service consequences or not. The ÖVP could have individual teachers punished, but never a large mass.


We stand in solidarity with the girls and the courageous teachers. One can only hope that this racist intervention can be averted. No one should be discriminated against because of their clothing. Resistance to racist bans is civil disobedience.
