Although the now applicable international law was formulated and codified as a lesson from the Second World War, its enforcement is still subject to the law of the stronger, the rules-based order of the USA, or the relativizing right of veto in the In(!)security Council.

Due to the constant and systematic violation of applicable international law by the veto powers and their appendages and the resulting de facto impunity, international law has been destroyed beyond recognition.

The peak of the codification of this impunity is probably the scandalous US law "The Hague Invasion Act", which authorizes the US military to invade The Hague, a NATO country in Europe, should US war criminals be convicted by the International Criminal Court and imprisoned there.

According to the slogan NO JUSTICE – NO PEACE, crimes of recent or more distant history must also be addressed to ensure justice for all. This particularly concerns the crimes of colonial countries such as mass murder, slavery, looting, robbery murder, land grabbing and theft. Without punishment and reparations there can be no justice and therefore no peace in the world.

Applicable international law could not even prevent a genocide in Gaza. This failure is an indication of the collapse of the international legal system as a whole. Its bias, its political subjugation to the Global West and its complicity in crimes committed by the Global West are now clearly exposed.

Yet the people who most urgently need the protection of international legal systems have little confidence in these mechanisms – and rightly so. But the principles of equality, freedom and liberation are still valid. If Gaza has proven how rotten the system is, hopefully its suffering will lead to a redefinition of what moral universalism means, and a strengthened Global South will promote the rebuilding of institutions and procedures that do justice to these principles.

This means that the world needs a powerful mechanism that can enforce compliance with international law without exception for everyone. Before international law, no one may be more equal than anyone else.

A start towards respecting international law can also be made by any body, including the human rights city of Graz, by taking a clear stand against all possible violations instead of joining the cowardly strategy of silence.
